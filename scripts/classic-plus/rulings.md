# Classic Plus: owner rulings

Decisions on questions the rulebook leaves open. They take precedence over
your own reading of the rules. The classifier reads this file before every
batch.

1. **Temporary banishing counts.** A card that banishes more than 1 opponent
   card "until the End Phase" (or similar) is mass removal (rule 8): the
   cards are off the field for the turn. A card that banishes a monster and
   returns it to the field later (including your own monster, "flicker")
   hits 7b: it returns a monster from banishment to the field. The rulebook
   decides only by (a)–(d), not by the heading.
2. **Draw with a cost is still draw.** "Put a card on the bottom of the Deck,
   then draw 1" and similar exchanges hit rule 5.
3. **"Your opponent cannot draw" counts as taking their Draw Phase** (rule 11),
   the same as "skips their Draw Phase".
4. **A lasting effect that punishes an opponent's action is a floodgate**
   (rule 3: restriction "durch Kosten"), e.g. "each time your opponent Special
   Summons, inflict 300 damage to them". Go through rule 3's check steps; it
   is not 6a.
5. **A monster's floodgate that only applies in certain situations** (e.g.
   "if this card attacks, your opponent cannot activate Spell/Trap Cards")
   is still a floodgate on a monster, which the opponent can get rid of by
   attacking it: step 3.2, unless step 3.1 applies first.
6. **A destruction substitute is protection.** "If it would be destroyed by
   battle, destroy this card instead" hits 9b; "… by a card effect, … instead"
   hits 9a.

## Second round (from the unsure flags of the full run)

### Rule 3 (floodgates)

7. **Attack-target restrictions are not floodgates.** "Your opponent can only
   attack this card", "cannot target X for attacks", or a forced attack
   *target* is not rule 3. Forcing attacks ("all monsters must attack") still
   is (restriction by compulsion).
8. **A lock on one single card is not a floodgate** ("that monster cannot
   attack or change its battle position"): rule 3 restricts a class of actions.
9. **Locks from activated or Trigger Effects:** one that lasts only "this turn"
   is not a floodgate; one that lasts while a card stays on the field is
   treated like a Continuous Effect.
10. **Card properties for step 3.3** are printed ones: Level, ATK/DEF, Type,
    Attribute, archetype, card name, card type (Spell/Trap/monster kind).
    Game states are not: how a monster was summoned, battle position, zone or
    column, counters.
11. **Step 3.2 applies to any lock whose continuation depends on a monster**
    (the monster itself, an equipped monster, "while you control X"), as long
    as the opponent gets rid of that monster by attacking it. It does not
    apply when that monster cannot be destroyed by battle or cannot be
    attacked; then steps 3.3 and 3.4 decide.
12. **Locks that affect both players are floodgates.**
13. **Continuous negation is rule 10 only**, not also a floodgate.
14. **Zone locks and "your cards cannot be negated/responded to" are
    floodgates; a revealed hand is not.**

### Rule 4 (hand traps)

15. **Preventing damage or granting protection from the hand is not
    "preventing an opponent's action".**
16. **A hand trap that removes your own card still hits rule 4** ("a card
    from any zone, except this card itself").

### Rule 5 (draw)

17. **Only draws for the card's controller count.** A card that makes only the
    opponent draw is not rule 5; "each player draws" is.
18. **Adding the top card of the Deck blind is a draw; excavating and choosing
    is a search.**

### Rule 6 (damage)

19. **A cost the controller pays does not make the damage a byproduct**
    (Tributing or sending your own card as cost): still 6a.
20. **Only destruction, a summon or a battle (including the attack
    declaration and damage calculation) make damage a byproduct.** Damage
    triggered by a discard, a card being sent to the GY, a negation, a flip or
    counters is primary (6a).
21. **Halving LP, setting LP to a value, and LP loss count as damage.**
22. **Reflecting or redirecting damage to the opponent is 6a.**
23. **Doubling (or otherwise multiplying) a monster's ATK is 6b.** Extra
    attacks, piercing damage and doubled effect damage are not 6b.

### Rule 7 (price circumvention)

24. **Equipping an opponent's monster to your own is 7a** (control over a card
    the opponent controlled).
25. **A monster from the GY placed as an Equip Card or a Spell is not 7b**; it
    does not return as a monster.
26. **A revival onto the opponent's field is still 7b.**
27. **Temporarily banishing an opponent's monster that then returns is 7b**
    (ruling 1).

### Rule 8 (mass removal)

28. **Using the opponent's monsters as material or Tributes counts as
    removal**; the exception covers only the activating player's own cards.
29. **Rule 8 is judged per resolution**: an effect that removes 1 card per
    activation is not rule 8, however often it can be activated.
30. **Taking control of several monsters is 7a, not rule 8.**

### Rule 9 (protection)

31. **Partial protection counts** (only against Spells, only once, only
    against some cards) if it protects against the opponent's cards; it does
    not if it only protects against your own or friendly cards.
32. **Protection of Spells, Traps or Field Spells is not rule 9**; rule 9 is
    about monsters.

### Rule 10 (negation)

33. **Negating only your own monster as a drawback is not rule 10** ("the
    summoned monster's effects are negated").
34. **Rewriting an effect ("the activated effect becomes …") is negation.**

### Rule 11 and the frequency modifier

35. **Skipping only Main Phase 2, or skipping straight to the End Phase,
    counts** for rule 11.
36. **A trigger without a limit, or "once per battle", that can activate more
    than once in the same turn is repeatable.**
